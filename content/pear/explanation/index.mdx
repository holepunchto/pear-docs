The pages here are for understanding, not doing. They answer "why" rather than "how": why does Pear use append-only logs, what does "peer-to-peer" mean in practice, what's the difference between Bare and Node, and why is there no central server.

If you want to build something, head to **[How To](/pear/how-to)**. If you need to look up a specific API, that's **[Reference](/pear/reference)**.

## In this section

Pages are grouped by topic and ordered roughly from foundational concepts to app-specific ones.

### Platform foundations

- [How the stack fits together](/p2p/explanation/how-the-stack-fits-together)—how Pear and Bare layer together, from the native C foundations up to the apps you run.
- [Runtime and languages](/pear/explanation/runtime-and-languages)—JavaScript on Bare, native addons for other languages, and the Pear-end pattern for cross-platform apps.
- [Using Bare on its own](/bare/explanation/use-bare-standalone)—the entry point for adopting Bare as a standalone runtime, without Pear's peer-to-peer platform.
- [Inside Bare](/bare/explanation/bare-runtime)—what the Bare runtime adds, its lifecycle, and why its standard library is opt-in.
- [Migrating from Node.js](/bare/explanation/migrating-from-nodejs)—what changes in your code and mental model when porting an app to Bare.
- [Peer-to-peer, demystified](/p2p/explanation/peer-to-peer-demystified)—hole punching, public-key identity, and the roles of [HyperDHT](/p2p/reference/building-blocks/hyperdht) and [Hyperswarm](/p2p/reference/building-blocks/hyperswarm).
- [One core, many platforms](/bare/explanation/bare-on-native)—running the peer-to-peer core in a Bare worklet behind a typed RPC seam.
- [Dependencies](/pear/explanation/dependencies-and-network)—why NPM is the install path and the runtime relationship to Node.

### Storing & replicating data

- [From append-only logs to files](/p2p/explanation/from-logs-to-files)—Hypercore, Hyperblobs, and [Hyperdrive](/p2p/reference/building-blocks/hyperdrive)—which storage layer to use and why.
- [Storage and distribution](/pear/explanation/storage-and-distribution)—where Pear keeps your data, what the swarm replicates, and how new releases reach users.
- [Availability and distribution](/pear/explanation/availability-and-blind-peering)—seeding an application bundle with `pear seed`, its limits, and finding the keys behind it.

### Building & shipping apps

- [Pear desktop application architecture](/pear/explanation/pear-desktop-architecture)—how a typical Pear Electron app splits updates, storage, and Bare workers (with a link to a full sample repo).
- [Workers](/pear/explanation/workers)—why peer-to-peer logic lives in a Bare worker behind one IPC stream, and where the host/worker boundary should sit.
- [Release pipeline](/pear/explanation/deployment-releasing-apps-p2p)—stage, provision, multisig, release lines, and deployment-directory diagrams (agnostic; sample implementation on GitHub).
